There's an early photo of me playing the flute when I'm roughly 8 years old and it has this freaky-looking curved head joint. This is actually a very common mechanic in flutes for children or just generally smaller players; it's also a standard for larger instruments like alto and contr'alto flutes. And for good reason – I borrowed my uncle's alto flute (he's 6'8") which comes with both a curved AND straight head (for if you're huge I guess, but I'm 5'4"), but even with the curved head it's cartoonishly big for me to the point where I joked that I can never play it in public because I look like a clown.

This got me thinking about how my curved head flute was probably one of my earlier experiences of encountering adaptive technology, and made me curious about the broader landscape of adaptive instruments. There are a lot of instruments that you can [control with your eyes](https://eyeharp.org/?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=11809231090&gbraid=0AAAAABzCDI4idI9yUt_y4CSBPi9NGJz-t&gclid=Cj0KCQjwi8nRBhDhARIsAHZf_pbAkdNjWd6_gBFf6VIyspMiIuHgJyJMm2UHIKp2DidjLxqNzDBS_ggaArjpEALw_wcB) or [small head movements](https://housemate.ie/magic-flute/), or people specializing in [building one-handed instruments](http://www.peterworrell.co.uk/onehandedinstruments.htm). There are a bunch of ergonomic drum thrones and a zillion forums about drumming with a herniated disc (I've been on them),to the point where there's such a thing as a [drummer injury specialists](https://www.painfreenyc.com/for-musicians/drummers/). I have chronic tendonitis in my wrists and at its worst I needed to wear these obscenely dorky but effective [tennis-elbow braces](https://www.ebay.com/itm/365857069942) for a while. Like jeez, everything hurts...

Then I started looking into just how big flutes can get. And the answer is gigantic, to the point where the sheer amount of air I imagine it must require to get a simple tone is almost supernatural. I think a lot about the way a flute is gendered, the homophobic characterizations of male flute players, and the pop cultural hypersexualization x freakification of wind instruments specifically (thank you to everyone who has ever invoked [*American Pie*](https://americanpie.fandom.com/wiki/Michelle_Flaherty) to my face, I was humiliated). So in this swirl of associations, there is something about flutes of this size and stature that feels almost like...socioculturally impossible, or even [situationally accelerated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Love_Boosters). They feel liminal or fantastic in the sense that they are the same in their essence but altered to a state that contradicts their essential connotation.
