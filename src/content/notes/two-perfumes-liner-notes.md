## You Died In My Dream Last Night

YDIMDLN opens with an improvisational flute solo that flows into an idyllic synthy dream state that takes a jolting turn. This track mirrors the escalation of a relationship from casual to something that has stakes to lose, which for me is often signaled when someone starts appearing in my dreams, and begs the question of whether something that feels good can be real.

YDIMDLN is a fully realized iteration a demo I made for a homework assignment in [Daniel Lopatin's School of Song workshop](https://schoolofsong.org/pages/daniel?shop_sign_in=true) in February 2026, which at least 15 mutual friends also took (I think SOS did a really huge IG marketing push in the week before the class). The assignment revolved around crafting "audentity," and the prompt was to chart the influences, intentions, and implementation methods that were specific to your musical self then make a song uniting them. I never fully honed my manifesto, but I took the prompt as a general exercise in how to communicate my sensibility as a drummer through music that centers the flute. 

I came up with this, which has corny sound design and no mix but I thought something about the essence was immediately compelling and worth keeping:

School of Song HW3, Feb 2026

The title "You Died In My Dream Last Night" is adapted from a poem by Heather Christle, *[My Love You Died In My Dream Last Night](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poetrymagazine/poems/1584143/my-love-you-died-in-my-dream-last-night)*.

## Two Perfumes

Two Perfumes is an exploration of rituals of excess (like wearing two perfumes, which I do) and breaking the fourth wall. I crunched the flute with distortion to invoke signal processing as a form of adornment, which can sometimes feel like disguise (a friend thought the distorted flute was a synth). Two Perfumes “covers” the flute, then gives itself away – a voice sample of me outlining the drum beat before producing it shatters the illusion on purpose – a humbling & humorous transparency of the effort behind effortlessness. Two Perfumes also has a sapphic connotation, obviously, but that was secondary.

The main percussive synth that the song opens with and plays throughout is a resampling of the flute stacks that enter at 0:33, which I recorded myself. I wanted the sense that you get flashes of something before becoming fully enveloped in it.

## Intimacy Gradient (Interlude)

The concept of an intimacy gradient is an architectural principle from *[A Pattern Language](https://patternlanguage.cc/Patterns/Intimacy-Gradient-(127))* that states the spaces in a building should be sequenced from most to least private. I've never read *A Pattern Language* and I know it's totally overdone in certain spheres yeah yeah okay I'm a poser but a bunch of my friends are architects so I've absorbed the concept socially and ambiently on the internet and I like to also think of it as an interpersonal channel between two people. It felt like an appropriate name for an interlude, like a hallway that takes you from one room to the next.

## Not Every Seagull is a Seagull

This title is pulled directly from a journal entry of mine recounting a day at [Fort Tilden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Tilden) with my childhood best friend, their new crush, and some of their friends:

***sun mar 8, 8:48pm, home***

*first warm day of the year so hopped in A’s subaru and walked the beach almost end to end. J brought two sets of binoculars & i learned that not everything that looks like a seagull is a seagull. we picked up banh mi on the way and while walking another queer approached us and asked to borrow a phone. A offered their portable charger instead and we wound up hanging with this person and sharing our sandwiches with them until their friend (who was also their ex, ofc) showed up and we all hung out a while. we noticed many thresholds on the beach of stark temperature shifts in the air, like stepping through the veil, and we determined it was because we were walking in and out of clouds (at one point we joked it was angels pissing in the air). the mist was low and the colors were saturated and the sky was stratified in a way i haven’t really seen, a straight blue line below the usual golden hour fare, no gradient. we had a lot of haunted encounters throughout the day with abandoned buildings and the whole group accompanied me (protectively & supportively) while i changed my tampon in a bush. everyone kept remarking that we were having a great day. i was so thirsty the whole time and my mouth got so salty from the mist. the beach was completely empty when we left and the light was hazy and A whispered, “did we die?” car ride home had a perfect sky, i played hildegard’s jour 1596 and everyone loved it (M identified an FKA twigs sample i never noticed) and C texted me “are you seeing the sky!!”*

This was one of those days that felt perfect but simple, where I felt grateful for a profound friendship that has stood the test of time and the ease I felt spending a day with their friends. We also have an eerie tendency to go through major transitions at the same time, including in our romantic relationships, and on this day we happened to be falling in love at the same time, but my crush wasn't there, so I just watched them flutter about with theirs. I went home and wrote a song, the original Not Every Seagull Is A Seagull that will never see the light of day, and the lyrics were this:

*me and my best friend we're always synced up*   

*we're on our periods, we broke up*    

*with the same archetype at the same time*    

*and now we both have crushes*    

*misty beach the first warm day in new york*   

*salt on my tongue, layers come off*    

*crush grabs their hand they're running ahead*   

*it's looking good for my friend*    

*and i hold the bags*    

*it feels good*    

*i watch the gulls*    

*it feels good*

## Kite Scene

First Kite Scene was a demo I made during the Great NYC Snowstorm of 2026, a Sunday afternoon in January weekend where I decided to spend my snow-in watching the entire *28 Days Later* franchise and playing the flute. This demo came together in about an hour, with 3 single-take improvisational flute lines layered on top of a synth loop. I wound up keeping basically the entire composition from those single takes in the final track and just beefed up the mix.

This song was also a technical milestone for me because in January I was also working through an incredibly beefy [mixing and mastering course](https://courses.seedtostage.com/p/mixing-mastering-course), like we're talking 50+ hours, and this is the first track I ever fully mixed myself to what I considered a release-ready extent.

P.S. There's also really good [Purelink song called Kite Scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sJKMhuAJ10s) and I went back and forth about whether to change the name but it feels too true, and having an abundance of musical kite scenes in the world is probably a good thing.

## Protector

Protector features a clip from a voicemail my mom left me in 2016 that makes me really sad. A lot of things happened in my adolescence through mid-20's that made it painful for me to exist in the world as myself and what I hear in her voice is resignation to the fact that no one including her could pull me out from where I was, I would have to do it myself. In that sense "Protector" is self-referential, the fact that the song exists is a triumph, and the distance between my self today and the receiver of the call is wide. But not so wide – I stay hyper-vigilant to protect myself from myself, and I inexplicably take a disproportionate amount of this onus on myself; my impulse in crisis is to disappear until I resolve it unwitnessed. I don't mean to end the project on a lonely note, but this project is inherently lonely. It exists because I stow away to "figure things out."
